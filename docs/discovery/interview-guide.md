# CoreChain discovery interview guide

## Goal

Understand one real workflow failure before proposing a solution. Seek examples, artefacts, and consequences—not general approval for CoreChain.

## Opening

> I am learning how drilling, core logging, sampling, laboratory results, and QA/QC work in practice. I am not asking for confidential information. I want to understand what is difficult, time-consuming, or risky in the current process.

## Core questions for a geologist or drilling-experienced participant

1. Can you walk me through a recent drillhole from core recovery until assay results were usable?
2. Who touches the information at each step: driller, core-yard staff, geologist, sample coordinator, laboratory, manager?
3. What do you record in the field, and where does each record go: paper, Excel, photos, messaging app, database, or another tool?
4. Where do delays, missing records, incorrect depths, duplicated work, or sample-ID mistakes happen most often?
5. Which record is hardest to trace back later: core box, geological interval, photograph, sample, dispatch, assay, or QA/QC control?
6. How are sample IDs generated, labels prepared, and custody handoffs documented?
7. How do laboratory results arrive, and what must be checked before they are trusted?
8. Which QA/QC controls and acceptance rules are essential in your work?
9. What would make a field geologist refuse to use a new tool?
10. If one part of the workflow could become reliable in 90 days, which part would be most valuable?

## Questions for an operations leader or mining engineer

1. When does poor drilling, sample, or assay information delay an operational or technical decision?
2. Which errors create the greatest cost, safety, compliance, or reporting risk?
3. Who owns the data and who is accountable for approving it?
4. What existing systems must any new tool work with, such as GEOVIA, Excel, laboratory files, GIS, or a company database?
5. What would justify paying for a workflow tool: less rework, faster assay turnaround, fewer missing samples, stronger auditability, or better reporting?
6. What security, hosting, approval, or procurement requirements would block adoption?

## Questions for an earth scientist or data-focused participant

1. Which geological or drillhole data sources do you rely on most?
2. How is data quality checked before it is used in interpretation or reporting?
3. Which information is routinely incomplete, inconsistent, or difficult to retrieve?
4. Which codes, coordinate systems, data dictionaries, and reporting standards must be preserved?
5. How should approved data reach modelling or mapping tools?

## Evidence to ask for

Ask only for non-confidential examples or a verbal walkthrough. Useful artefacts include a blank core log, sample register, dispatch form, assay CSV layout, QA/QC summary, or a screenshot with sensitive information removed.

## Close

> Based on what you shared, my current understanding is that the biggest issue is [repeat their wording]. Is that accurate? May I follow up after I turn this into a small workflow concept?
