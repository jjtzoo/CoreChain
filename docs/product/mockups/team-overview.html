Team overview: where does the programme stand?

A resident or project manager should be able to open one screen and know where each hole stands, what is waiting to go to the laboratory, and whose phone has not reported, without messaging a single geologist. It is read-only: the geologists' records stay theirs.

Hole names and planned depths are real, from the Alberta sample project (Alberta Geological Survey, AGS DIG 2024-0022). Everything else is illustrative: metres logged, recovery, samples, people and times. The real survey logged only selected intervals, so it would not show a programme in progress. Screens use the web app's own colours.

2 · Hole view

Every lane on one depth axis, so gaps show themselves

Click a hole and the same evidence the geologist sees appears as lanes along the hole: drilled, boxed, photographed, logged, sampled. A gap in any lane is a job not done, or work still on a phone that has not synced. The manager reads it; they do not edit it.

What it deliberately does not do

  • It does not edit. A manager sees a geologist's records; changing them is the geologist's job, so the history stays honest.
  • It never shows a number without its age. Every figure is as of a phone's last sync, and the screen says so.
  • It does not score people. "Who logged what" is for tracing evidence, not a league table.
  • It does not replace the phone. Nothing here is needed in the field; the phone works with no signal.

How we will know it works

The test is simple: on a working day, can a manager say where every hole stands, and which phones are behind, without asking anyone?

  • Status questions sent to geologists per week. Should fall towards none. Counted by asking the manager during the field test.
  • Time to answer "where do things stand?". Target: under a minute from opening the page. A design target, not yet measured.
  • How often "as of" is the reason a number was not trusted. Tells us whether sync age needs to be louder.

Questions for a resident or project manager

  • 1What do you look at first each morning, and what do you ask geologists that this screen should already answer?
  • 2Is percentage of planned depth logged the right progress measure, or would you rather see metres drilled, logged and sampled side by side?
  • 3What makes you trust a number? Is "as of the phone's last sync" enough, or do you need to see what is missing?
  • 4Who else needs this view: the competent person, the laboratory, an inspector? Should they see the same screen?
  • 5Which reports do you assemble by hand today, and from which tables?
